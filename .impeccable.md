## Design Context

### Users
Meal.OS is for busy Indian households coordinating next-day meals with a cook or a shared household cooking workflow.

The product is most often used during a short evening planning window on mobile, with occasional desktop use for review, admin tasks, and public demo screenshots.

The core job is to turn a quick nightly check-in about vegetables, leftovers, and household preferences into tomorrow's executable cooking plan.

### Brand Personality
Meal.OS should feel editorial, operational, and composed.

The interface should communicate confidence, calm, and product maturity rather than playfulness or novelty. It should feel like a reliable household operating layer, not a lifestyle app, recipe blog, or generic AI assistant.

### Aesthetic Direction
The visual direction is editorial-operational, light-mode first, restrained, and premium.

Use strong typography, crisp hierarchy, deliberate spacing, and a small number of polished surfaces that look credible in both mobile and desktop screenshots.

India-first identity should come through the workflow, language, and food context rather than decorative motifs, clichéd warm palettes, or ornamental cultural references.

Anti-references:
- stereotypical "India-themed" UI styling
- generic neon AI aesthetics
- cluttered internal-tool dashboards
- ad-heavy landing-page copy
- repeating the same product promise across every task screen

### Design Principles
1. Lead with operational clarity.
   Every important screen should make the next household decision obvious within a few seconds.
2. Make outputs feel artifact-grade.
   Plans, briefs, and shopping lists should feel like polished deliverables, not raw app state.
3. Show domain specificity through behavior, not decoration.
   The product should feel India-first because of what it helps with, not because of visual clichés.
4. Keep task screens focused.
   The home screen can explain the system, but workflow screens should stay compact and execution-oriented.
5. Optimize for screenshot credibility.
   Layout, hierarchy, and copy should read cleanly at a glance for hackathon judges, public repo visitors, and contributors.
